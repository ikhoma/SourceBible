# Content Creation Skill — SourceBible

Guidelines and frameworks for creating effective marketing content for **SourceBible**, across channels. This skill is tuned to SourceBible's brand; the brand layer below governs every piece of content, and the channel frameworks that follow it are the mechanics.

---

## Brand layer (apply to everything)

### What SourceBible is
A native iOS Bible-study app that turns *reading* into *understanding*. It links every verse to its original Hebrew and Greek, a clear lexicon, cross-references, morphology, usage across Scripture, and historic commentary — all reachable in one tap, without leaving the page you're reading.

### Slogan / north-star line
**Bridge the gap between modern readers and the world of the biblical text.**

Use this as the anchor message. Headlines and hero copy should ladder up to it. Don't overuse it verbatim in body copy — let the *idea* (bridging reading and understanding) carry through.

### The core positioning
> Most Bible apps are built for reading. SourceBible is built for understanding.

Every piece of content should, somewhere, make the reading→understanding contrast feel real.

### Audience
- **Primary:** Christians who study seriously — small-group leaders, pastors, teachers, people preparing sermons or lessons.
- **Secondary:** motivated readers who want to go deeper than their current Bible app, and who are **not** trained in Hebrew or Greek.
- Write for high motivation, mobile-first, and no assumption of original-language training. Make depth feel approachable, never academic or gatekept.

### Voice and tone
- **Warm, reverent, and confident** — never gimmicky, never hype-y, never clickbait. This is a faith product; respect the subject and the reader.
- **Clear over clever.** Plain language. Short sentences. No jargon unless it's immediately explained (e.g., "Strong's numbers — the IDs that link a word to its original meaning").
- **Curiosity-driven.** Lead with a real question a reader would ask, then show the answer is one tap away.
- **Inclusive of beginners.** Never imply the reader should already know Greek/Hebrew. The promise is "you don't need training to go deep."
- Avoid: salesy urgency, fear-based hooks, exclamation-heavy copy, and anything that mocks other apps or denominations. Stay non-sectarian.

### The signature framework: reader's question → tool that answers it
This is SourceBible's most distinctive content pattern. **Prefer it whenever you present features.** Frame each capability as the natural question a reader already has, paired with the tool that answers it — not as a feature spec.

| A reader's question | The tool that answers it |
|---|---|
| What else does the Bible say about this? | Cross-references |
| Why do translations differ here? | Translation comparison |
| What word is in the original? | Lexicon |
| What does this word mean? | Word meaning |
| Why does it take this particular form? | Morphology |
| How is it connected to the other words in the sentence? | Syntax |
| How is this word used elsewhere in Scripture? | Usage / concordance |

Rules for this framing:
- Lead with the **need (the question)**, then the **feature that solves it** — in that order.
- Phrase questions in the reader's own voice ("What's the real word here?"), not marketing voice.
- Keep the feature side short and concrete.
- Use it in feature grids, value-prop sections, social carousels, email feature blocks, and "how it works" content.

### Approved vocabulary
- Say **"study," "understand," "explore," "go deeper," "the original text/words," "bridge."**
- Two study modes: **Verse mode** (context) and **Word mode** (original language).
- Call it **SourceBible** (one word). Not "Source Bible."
- Describe the experience as **"one tap, never lose your place"** — the study sheet that opens over your reading and returns you exactly where you were.

### Claims discipline
SourceBible is built and in testing; it is **pre-public-launch**. Do **not** invent adoption numbers, user counts, ratings, or testimonials. If social proof is needed and none exists yet, use the mission and the product capabilities as proof instead, or use clearly-labeled placeholders for the team to fill in. Keep all scriptural and scholarly claims accurate; commentary sources are public-domain theologians.

## Content Type Templates

> For every template below, default to the brand voice above and use the **question → tool** framing wherever features appear.

### Blog Post Structure
1. **Headline** — clear, benefit-driven, includes primary keyword (aim for 60 characters or less for SEO). For SourceBible, lead with a study question or the reading→understanding idea.
2. **Introduction** (100-150 words) — hook with a real reader's question or a relatable study moment. State what the post will cover. Include primary keyword.
3. **Body sections** (3-5 sections) — each with a descriptive subheading (H2). One core idea per section with supporting evidence, examples, or a concrete verse walkthrough.
4. **Conclusion** (75-100 words) — reinforce reading→understanding, include a call to action.
5. **Meta description** — under 160 characters, includes primary keyword, compels the click.

*SourceBible blog angles that work:* a single word's meaning unpacked (e.g., "What 'hesed' really means"), why two translations of one verse differ, how to study a passage in 10 minutes, original-language insight for sermon prep.

### Social Media Post Structure
- **Hook** — open with a reader's question or a surprising original-language fact.
- **Body** — 2-4 concise points, or a mini word-study payoff.
- **CTA** — what to do next (comment, save, try it in SourceBible).
- **Hashtags** — 3-5 relevant (e.g., #BibleStudy #Scripture #Greek #Hebrew #SermonPrep), platform-dependent.

*Format tip:* the question → tool table makes excellent carousel/thread content — one question per slide, the answer on the reveal.

### Email Newsletter Structure
- **Subject line** — under 50 characters; a study question performs well ("What does this word really mean?").
- **Preview text** — complements the subject, doesn't repeat it.
- **Header/hero** — one-line value statement tied to the slogan.
- **Body sections** — 2-3 scannable blocks; a recurring "word of the week" or "verse behind the verse" works well.
- **Primary CTA** — one clear action (open the app to a specific passage).
- **Footer** — unsubscribe, company info, social links.

### Landing Page Structure
- **Headline** — the slogan or a tight variant, primary benefit in under 10 words.
- **Subheadline** — elaborate: original languages, lexicon, cross-references, commentary, one tap, never lose your place.
- **Hero section** — headline, subheadline, primary CTA, app screenshot/video.
- **Value propositions** — 3-4 benefit blocks (reading meets understanding; one tap, never lost; original language made approachable; depth on your terms).
- **Feature grid** — use the question → tool framing directly.
- **Social proof** — testimonials/logos/stats *when they exist*; until then, lead with mission and capability.
- **Objection handling** — FAQ ("Do I need to know Greek or Hebrew?" → No.).
- **Final CTA** — repeat the primary call to action.

### Press Release Structure
- **Headline** — factual, newsworthy, under 80 characters.
- **Subheadline** — optional context.
- **Dateline** — city, date.
- **Lead paragraph** — who, what, when, where, why in 2-3 sentences.
- **Body paragraphs** — supporting details, a founder quote, context on the reading→understanding gap.
- **Boilerplate** — use the approved boilerplate below.
- **Media contact** — name, email.

**Approved boilerplate:** *SourceBible is a mobile Bible-study app that bridges the gap between modern readers and the original biblical text. By linking every verse to its Hebrew and Greek source, a clear lexicon, cross-references, and historic commentary — all in one uninterrupted flow — SourceBible turns passive reading into genuine understanding.*

### Case Study Structure
- **Title** — "[Reader/leader] goes deeper in Scripture with SourceBible" (avoid fabricated metrics).
- **Snapshot** — who they are, how they study, what changed (callout box).
- **Challenge** — the study friction they faced (fragmented tools, no bridge to the original).
- **Solution** — how SourceBible's flow solved it, framed as questions answered.
- **Results** — qualitative outcomes where quantitative aren't available; never invent numbers.
- **Quote** — real testimonial only.
- **CTA** — "Start studying free."

## Writing Best Practices by Channel

### Blog
- Write at an accessible reading level; explain any Hebrew/Greek or scholarly term on first use.
- Short paragraphs (2-4 sentences); subheadings every 200-300 words.
- Anchor abstract points in a concrete verse or word example.
- Active voice; front-load key information.

### Social Media
- **LinkedIn**: thoughtful, ministry/leadership angle, sermon-prep value; ~1,300 characters before "see more."
- **Twitter/X**: punchy word-studies and translation comparisons; threads for passage walkthroughs.
- **Instagram**: visual-first — verse typography, original-language reveals; line breaks for readability; hashtags at end or first comment.
- **Facebook**: conversational, community-oriented; questions drive comments.

### Email
- Subject lines built on curiosity or clear study value; avoid manufactured urgency.
- Personalize where possible.
- One primary CTA, visually distinct.
- Scannable: bold key phrases, short paragraphs.
- Mobile-first.

### Web (Landing Pages, Product Pages)
- Lead with benefits (understanding), not feature names.
- Use "you" language.
- Every section answers "so what?" for a reader who wants to understand Scripture.
- Reduce friction; trust signals near CTAs ("Free," "No account required to start" — only if true).

## SEO Fundamentals for Content

### Keyword Strategy
- One primary keyword + 2-3 secondary keywords per piece. Relevant SourceBible territory: "Bible study app," "original Hebrew and Greek Bible," "Strong's concordance app," "interlinear Bible app," "study the Bible in its original language," "lexicon Bible app."
- Use the primary keyword in: headline, first paragraph, one subheading, meta description, URL slug.
- Write for humans first; never keyword-stuff.

### On-Page SEO Checklist
- Title tag: under 60 characters, primary keyword.
- Meta description: under 160 characters, primary keyword, compels click.
- URL slug: short, descriptive, primary keyword.
- One H1; descriptive H2/H3 with secondary keywords where natural.
- Descriptive image alt text.
- 2-3 internal links; 1-2 authoritative external links.

### Content-SEO Integration
- Aim for comprehensive topic coverage (depth is rewarded).
- Answer related questions ("People Also Ask").
- Structure for featured snippets: definition paragraphs, numbered lists, and the question → tool table.

## Headline and Hook Formulas

### Headline Formulas (SourceBible-flavored)
- **The word behind the word** — "What 'love' really says in the original Greek"
- **Why [translations] differ** — "Why Your Two Bibles Don't Agree on This Verse"
- **[Achieve study result] without [obstacle]** — "Study Scripture in Its Original Words — No Greek Required"
- **The [adjective] way to [study result]** — "The Simplest Way to Study the Bible's Original Language"
- **[Reader's question], answered in one tap** — "What does this word mean? Answered in one tap."

### Hook Formulas (Opening Lines)
- **Reader's question**: "You're reading a verse and you wonder — what's the actual word here?"
- **Reading→understanding contrast**: "You've read this verse a hundred times. You've never seen what's underneath it."
- **Surprising original-language fact**: "The word translated 'patience' here literally means 'long-nosed.'"
- **Relatable friction**: "Three apps, two tabs, and you've lost your place again."
- **Scenario**: "It's Saturday night, the sermon's half-written, and you need to know what this word meant to its first readers."

## Call-to-Action Best Practices

### CTA Principles
- Action verbs: "Start," "Study," "Explore," "See," "Try."
- Be specific: "Start studying free" beats "Sign up."
- Reduce risk honestly: "Free to start," "No account required" — only if accurate.
- One primary CTA per page or email.

### CTA Examples by Context
- **Blog post**: "Open this passage in SourceBible" / "Subscribe for a word-study each week"
- **Landing page**: "Start studying free" / "See how it works"
- **Email**: "Read the word behind the verse" / "Open in SourceBible"
- **Social media**: "Save this for your next study" / "What word do you want unpacked next?"
- **Case study**: "Start studying free" / "See how SourceBible works"

### CTA Placement
- Above the fold on landing pages.
- After establishing value in emails.
- At the end of blog posts.
- In-line when contextually relevant.
- Repeat the primary CTA at the bottom of long pages.
