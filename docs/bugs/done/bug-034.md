# Description

Six OT books had **no BibleHub transliterations at all** — 4,807 verses across
1 Samuel, 2 Samuel, 1 Kings, 2 Kings, 1 Chronicles and 2 Chronicles. They fell
back to the per-token `xlit`, so the app showed a worse transliteration rather
than a wrong one, and nobody noticed for two months.

## Steps to Reproduce

1. Open 1 Sam. 1:1 → Word → Original.
2. Compare the transliteration quality against, say, Genesis 1:1.

## Expected

Every OT book has slot-level transliterations in `word.xlit_slot`.

## Actual

`xlit_slot` was NULL for all 4,807 verses in those six books.

## Root cause

`OT_SLUGS` spelled the numbered books with a hyphen:

```python
"1SA": "1-samuel",  "1KI": "1-kings",  "1CH": "1-chronicles",   # wrong
```

BibleHub uses an underscore — `1_samuel`. Every request 404'd, `fetch()` retried
three times, gave up and returned `None`, and the loop moved on **silently**. No
counter, no log line, no build assertion. Every other book, including `songs`
for Song of Solomon, was spelled correctly, which is why the failure clustered so
cleanly on exactly the books whose slug starts with a digit.

The measurement that exposed it: 23,213 Macula OT verses, 18,240 with BibleHub
data, 4,973 missing — and 4,807 of the missing sat in those six books.

## Fix

One character per entry in `OT_SLUGS`, in
`scripts/fetch_biblehub_translit_hebrew_v2.py`:

```python
"1SA": "1_samuel",  "1KI": "1_kings",  "1CH": "1_chronicles",
```

Then a re-fetch of 4,970 pages (~66 min). Result: 4,965 stored, 4 refused by the
Strong's guard, 4 network failures — all four later recovered by `--repair`.

Verified by canaries in the audit: `1SA 1:1` now reads `way-hî ’îš ’e-ḥāḏ min-`
and `1CH 1:1` reads `’ā-ḏām šêṯ ’ĕ-nō-wōš`, both agreement 1.00.

## Notes

The real defect is not the typo — it is that a whole book scoring zero coverage
was not an error condition. A silent `None` from a network helper became a
permanently empty book.

Guard added: the audit in `fetch_biblehub_translit_hebrew_v2.py --verify` now
counts Macula verses with **no entry at all** and fails on any of them. Before
that change it scored only stored verses, so a purged or never-fetched verse was
invisible to it — an audit that could report "every stored verse agrees" while
30 verses had quietly ceased to exist.

Regression test: `test_audit_counts_verses_that_have_no_entry` in
`scripts/tests/test_translit_pipeline.py`.
